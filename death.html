A KINGDOM IN MOURNING

HIS MAJESTY KING HAMLET IS DEAD

Beloved sovereign found lifeless in the royal orchard; court attributes passing to a venomous serpent.

News from the Castle

With great sorrow, the Royal Court of Elsinore announces the passing of His Majesty King Hamlet, whose reign was marked by strength and steadfast devotion to Denmark.

His Majesty was discovered in the royal orchard, where he had retired to take his customary rest. According to the statement released by the court, a serpent's bite brought about his sudden and most unfortunate death.

The palace has requested that the people of Denmark observe a period of mourning.

[An accompanying dispatch could not be recovered.]

❦

Official Notice

By order of the Royal Court, all public celebrations are to be suspended during the days of mourning. The bells of Elsinore shall toll in remembrance of the late King.

❦

This account has been issued for public circulation by the Office of Royal Communications.

A Court in Silence

Queen Gertrude and Prince Hamlet have withdrawn from public engagements as the household grieves its loss. Courtiers are instructed to refrain from speculation regarding the circumstances of the King's death.

The court's account identifies the serpent as the cause. No further particulars concerning the discovery have been made available for publication.

In the absence of additional testimony, the Chronicle reproduces the official statement as received.

Notice to Readers

Requests for further information should be addressed to the Royal Court. The Chronicle cannot independently verify details withheld by the palace.
